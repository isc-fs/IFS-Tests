# Integrity report guide

For the Technical Directors and the maintainer who review fair play in ranked play (the daily question and counted mock runs). `ifs-tests integrity-report` is a **read-only forensics report** over the attempts already stored: it prints tables, decides nothing, changes nothing and stores nothing. A person reads it, and a person decides whether to talk to someone. The rules it checks against (what is allowed in ranked play and what is not) and the decision to run it are the Technical Directors' and are recorded in ADR 0010; the notice members read says the same in English and Spanish.

The server side (the script, the lock, the read-only role) is in [runbook 5.7](../runbook.md#57-integrity-report); the controls are in [security.md](../security.md#threats-and-controls). This page is how to run the report and how to read it.

## Purpose and limits

- **Three kinds of output.** Cohort and bank checks (`elo`, `labels`, `permutation`, `keys`, `fidelity`) describe groups and questions and print no person at all. The per-person checks (`follow`, `upstream-key`, `profile`, `home-away`, `canary`, `people`, `split`) print **pseudonyms** only. A name appears in one place, `integrity-reveal`, and only for the pseudonyms you type.
- **Per-person checks run only on answers from the rule date.** The cohort checks fit purposes the app already states (leaderboards, bank quality). The per-person ones are screening of individuals for a new purpose, so ADR 0010 limits them to answers given on or after the season-1b rule date (`IFS_RULES_1B_FROM`), when the bilingual notice is live and the team has been told. The command enforces it: without the date, or with `--since` before it, a per-person check is refused ([Before you run](#before-you-run)). The cohort checks can still read the days before it.
- **A flag is a reason for a conversation, not a finding.** "Worth a conversation" needs at least two of four independent-ish evidence families to agree ([the two-family rule](#the-two-family-rule)). Nothing is decided automatically.
- **No flag does not mean clean.** At about three graded answers per person per day the statistical power against light use is close to nil, and careful use of an AI assistant (reading the answer, typing it a minute later) is undetectable in this data. Only a supervised, in-room benchmark (check 5, a live session with everyone answering without access to the question on their own device) measures it. The report cannot replace it. Check 5 is `supervised` in the people table and always prints `pending`.
- **The canary shows that a note was followed, not that an AI was used.** `canary` ([Reading `canary`](#reading-canary)) counts how often a player picks the decoy letter after copying a long piece of a question. A hit is "followed the note", by an AI assistant or by a person nudged by it, and the test tolerates a configured share of nudged readers. `IFS_CANARY` is the pilot's on/off switch for drawing a decoy at all, not a date, and the report does not read it: with it off the section says `none in the window` and the context columns are still filled. Before the rule date the signals print as `not collected`.
- **The signals are context, never a check.** Time away, pastes, long copies and reopenings ([Context columns](#context-columns)) print beside the people the other families already surfaced. They are forgeable, they come from allowed behaviour as often as from help, and they never set a family or put anyone in the table: `families()` does not read them, so they cannot be the second family of a conversation.
- **One switch, one release.** Everything in the wave (the signals, the decoy, the per-player daily, the per-person window of this report) ships in v2.0.0, deployed dark before the rule date, and switches on together at Madrid midnight of `IFS_RULES_1B_FROM`. Before it the app behaves as it did and the default (cohort) report runs. There is no date setting per mechanism.
- **The sample starts at the rule date.** See below.

## Before you run

1. **The per-person window starts at the rule date.** Per-person checks (`follow`, `upstream-key`, `profile`, `home-away`, `canary`, `people`, `split`) need `IFS_RULES_1B_FROM` in the environment (a Madrid day, `YYYY-MM-DD`). It comes from the environment's `.env` through `deploy/compose.yaml`, like the app's, and is not in `integrity.env`; a blank value counts as unset and a malformed one is reported under that exact name. The command refuses when:
   - the date is unset (the message names it and ADR 0010);
   - `--since` is before the date: answers given before it are never a reason for a step about a person;
   - the window then holds no answer from the rule date (`--since`, which defaults to the later of the season's 1 September and the rule date, is not before `--until`).

   Cohort-only runs behave as before, and may cover the days before the date (ADR 0010 asks for the relay size before it as a cohort measurement). The default `--checks` is `cohort`, so a plain run needs no rule date; `--checks all` includes the per-person checks and exits before the date. The rule date is the one switch for the whole release: collection, the signals' effective day and this window. The canary has no date of its own; `IFS_CANARY` only decides whether a decoy is drawn.
   The canary walk starts at `max(rule date, the season's 1 September)`: in 2026-27 that is the rule date, and from 1 September 2027 on it is the season's start, so a season-long window is still the full walk and not exploratory (see [Reading `canary`](#reading-canary)).
2. **`--until` is the last day and is excluded.** Plays submitted from that day's Madrid midnight on are left out, and it can't be in the future: the window is closed, so a rerun gives the same answer. Account deletion takes a person's attempts with it ([ADR 0006](../adr/0006-personal-data.md)) and the backups drop them within 14 days. Nothing is kept to the contrary.
3. **The window is one season.** `--since` can't be earlier than the season's 1 September: the September rank reset would apply to a longer window. A window spanning 1 September is refused. The LP replay always walks from the season start; `--since` only chooses which plays are reported.
4. **One salt per review**, generated and kept by the reviewer who is **not** the operator (their password manager): at least 32 characters, for example `openssl rand -hex 32`. It is typed by that reviewer at the `Salt (typed by the reviewer who holds it):` prompt, so the operator running the script never sees it on screen (it is not echoed). It lives in the script's shell for that run and reaches the container on standard input, so an operator who wanted to could read it from that process while it runs: the separation is procedural ([the two-person reveal](#the-two-person-reveal)). The same salt must be used for `integrity-reveal` later, and it is destroyed when the review closes. It is never in `.env`, in the repository or in the shell history. (`IFS_INTEGRITY_SALT` and `--salt-stdin` exist for tests and automation; don't use them on the server.)
5. **The thresholds file**, `/srv/quiz/<env>/integrity.env`, `chmod 600`, one `NAME=value` per line (comments allowed). The decision thresholds have **no defaults anywhere in the repository**, because the repository is public and a value in it is a value a player can aim at. The report refuses to run without every one of them (the seven canary and context ones only for `canary`, `people` and `split`, below; a cohort-only run needs none of them), naming all the missing `IFS_INTEGRITY_*` variables in one message and, separately, any whose value is out of range as `invalid <NAME>` (`α`, `β`, `f` and `cα` outside `(0, 1)`, `α + β ≥ 1`, `cα < α`, `g0` outside `[0, f)`, `M` below 1 or not an integer, a negative away threshold, a malformed `IFS_RULES_1B_FROM` named without the prefix), and never prints their values (its header says `thresholds: from config`; the text says "the configured window" and "n >= min_n", never the number). The script asks for the salt before the container checks the file, so check the file first (`grep -c IFS_INTEGRITY_ /srv/quiz/<env>/integrity.env` should say 21). Keep the real values in the Technical Directors' notes, not in this repository, in the output or in chat.

   | Variable | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_MIN_N` | First-seen plays a person needs before performance and timing are judged; below it the family is `insufficient`, not negative |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_FOLLOW_WITHIN_MIN` | Minutes: two players form a copy pair on a daily question when they submitted within this long of each other |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_FOLLOW_MIN` | Questions a pair needs before it is tested: both wrong (copy), or the leader wrong before the follower answered (relay) |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_FOLLOW_Z` | Copy: the one-sided level, as a z, of the same wrong answer more often than chance; Holm over every copy pair tested |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_FOLLOW_RELAY_Z` | Relay: the one-sided level, as a z, of the follower being right where an earlier player was wrong more often than their own other answers predict; Holm over every relay tested |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_READING_FLOOR_S` | Seconds below which an answer is faster than anyone reads the question |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_READING_CPS` | Reading speed in characters per second; the floor for a question is the larger of the fixed floor and its text length divided by this |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_BELOW_FLOOR_SHARE` | Share of a person's first-seen answers under the floor that counts as the timing signal |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_NARROW_SD_LOG` | Standard deviation of the log of the answer time at or below which the latency profile counts as narrow |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_FLAT_CORR` | Absolute rank correlation between answer time and question length at or below which the profile counts as length-insensitive |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_HARD_RESIDUAL_Z` | z of the person's right answers on first-seen questions of difficulty 4 and 5 against what their own first-seen answers on easier questions predict |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_AWAY_Z` | One-sided z of a person doing better on away-area questions than on home-area ones |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_UPSTREAM_MIN` | Corrected-key questions first seen after the correction that a person needs before the upstream-key test |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_UPSTREAM_Z` | z (from the exact tail) of a person matching the old upstream key more often than the rest of the cohort |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_ALPHA` | Canary: `0 < α < 1`, the false-reject level over the season: a person is rejected when their likelihood-ratio walk reaches `M/α` (Bonferroni over the fixed `M`). Needed with `canary`, `people` or `split` |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_BETA` | Canary: `0 < β < 1` with `α + β < 1`; places the `no sign at f` label and sets the power floor of the controls. Flags no one. Needed with `canary`, `people` or `split` |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_FOLLOW` | Canary: the alternative to chance, `0 < f < 1`: the share of a follower's wrong answers on copy trials that go to the decoy. Needed with `canary`, `people` or `split` |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_CONTROL_ALPHA` | Canary: `α ≤ cα < 1`, the level of the draw check, the pooled and the own placebo and the nudge warning, separate from `α` so that a strict `α` does not make the controls blind to a leak. Needed with `canary`, `people` or `split` |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_NUDGE` | Canary: `0 ≤ g0 < f`, the share of an honest reader's wrong answers after a copy that go to the decoy beyond chance (a translator user, say); the walk tests against `p0 + g0(1 − p0)`. Needed with `canary`, `people` or `split` |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_M` | Canary: an integer `M ≥ 1`, the number of people the boundary `ln(M/α)` is spread over, fixed for the season; more people with copy trials than this makes every decision `ignored`. Needed with `canary`, `people` or `split` |
   | `IFS_INTEGRITY_AWAY_UNDECLARED_S` | Seconds (`≥ 0`) of undeclared time away from one answer above which the context column counts it as `over`. Needed with `canary`, `people` or `split` |

   The neutral settings have defaults and can stay out of the file: `IFS_INTEGRITY_Z` (1.96, the width of every Wilson interval), `IFS_INTEGRITY_MIN_GROUP` (3), `IFS_INTEGRITY_PERMUTATION_ROUNDS` (5000), `IFS_INTEGRITY_BOOTSTRAP_ROUNDS` (2000), `IFS_INTEGRITY_SEED` (0), `IFS_INTEGRITY_FIXED_RANK` (350, overridden by `--fixed-rank`) and `IFS_INTEGRITY_SHRINK_M` (5).

   **Choosing the values is a calibration, not a guess and not a quantile.** Run the cohort checks and `--checks profile` first: the profile section prints the cohort's p10, p50 and p90 of median answer time, below-floor share and spread of log time *before* any flag, so you can see what ordinary play looks like here. Set the reading speed and floor from what no one reads that fast, and the z thresholds from how many false flags you are willing to expect (the people table's footer prints, per family, the flags chance alone would give). Every z in the report is the one-sided z of an exact or predictive p-value, so `2.5` means a chance of about 0.6 % per test, whatever the number of items. Don't set a threshold at a cohort percentile: with about 70 people a 95th-percentile gate flags three or four of them by construction, cheating or not. Write the values and the date in the review notes.

   **The canary values.** `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_ALPHA` is the false-reject level over the season for up to `M` people, not per run: the boundary `ln(M/α)` is fixed, so it does not move between runs. `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_BETA` places the `no sign at f` label and the power floor of the controls; it flags no one. `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_CONTROL_ALPHA` is at least `α`: the level of the controls (a strict `α` must not blind them to a leak). The follow share comes from the recall pilot: with `F` the share of pilot runs in which the AI followed the note, `u` the share of copies you expect to go to an AI assistant, `w_ai` the rate at which the assistant is wrong when it does not follow, and `w_h` the rate at which the person is wrong without it, the share of a follower's wrong answers that go to the decoy is

   `f = u·F / (u·F + u·(1−F)·w_ai + (1−u)·w_h)`

   Set `f` at the part-time follower you want to catch, not the full-time one: a larger `f` charges each miss more, so a player who follows only some of the time is flagged late or not at all, and a smaller `f` needs more hits to flag anyone but forgives misses. `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_NUDGE` (`g0`) is the pilot's upper confidence bound on the share of an honest reader's wrong answers after a copy that go to the decoy beyond chance, in the same units as `f`; it is what keeps "followed the note" from meaning "read their translation", and it must stay below `f`. `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_M` is at least the number of people who can play ranked this season and is fixed for the season: a larger `M` costs power, and fewer than the people who show up makes every decision `ignored`. Take `IFS_INTEGRITY_AWAY_UNDECLARED_S` from the cohort table of the context columns after the first weeks. Don't publish any of the seven: a published away threshold tells a player how long they can stay away without showing up in the column, and a published `f`, `g0` or `α` is a distance they can keep. Trials on 3-option questions carry about half the evidence per trial of 4-option ones; trials with more options carry more.
6. **Pseudonymous is not anonymous.** The pseudonym is `HMAC-SHA256(salt, user id)` cut to 8 hex characters. Anyone with the salt and the user ids can map everyone. The report prints no LP, vertical, position, dates or per-question rows next to a pseudonym so that it can't be re-identified by itself, but `n` is printed beside every rate (it has to be: a rate without `n` is not evidence), and in a team of about 70 a rare combination can still point at someone. Treat the output as personal data.

## Where it runs

On the **server only**, through `deploy/integrity-report.sh`, as the `backup_ro` role with its own connection, never the app's. **Never on a laptop** (a copy of the data on a laptop is the thing the design avoids), and **never `deploy/restore.sh`** or `deploy/refresh-bank.sh` to "get a copy to look at": a restore overwrites the environment it runs against.

- The script takes the same lock as a deploy and a restore (`operation.lock`), so it never overlaps one and refuses while one runs. It reads its transaction snapshot in seconds; avoid 00:01 (daily pick) and 03:00 to 04:00 (maintenance, backup).
- It opens **one** database connection (no pool), so it fits the budget the [runbook](../runbook.md#5-everyday-operations) counts for a command run alongside the app (that budget counts 10 for a pooled command; this one uses 1). The connection is read-only at three levels: the role has no write privilege, the transaction is `READ ONLY` and `REPEATABLE READ`, and the server stops a statement after 120 s, a lock wait after 5 s and an idle transaction after 60 s.
- The report is computed after the transaction is closed, so nothing waits on it.

## Staging first

Run the same command on staging before prod. That checks the mechanics, not the findings:

1. The connection works as `backup_ro` (the password arrives as `PGPASSWORD`; there is no fallback to the app's DSN) and the run finishes in reasonable time. While it runs, `docker exec quiz-staging-db-1 psql -U postgres -d quiz -c "select usename, state, left(query, 40) from pg_stat_activity where usename = 'backup_ro'"` shows one connection and only `SELECT` or `WITH` statements.
2. `operation.lock` exists during the run and is gone after; starting the script while `deploy/deploy.sh staging` runs is refused.
3. The report file is `0600` under `/dev/shm`, and `grep -E '@|display_name|[0-9]{6,}' <file>` finds nothing, nor does a grep for each value in `integrity.env`.
4. Without `integrity.env` the script refuses; with the file but a threshold missing or not a number, the command exits naming it as missing or invalid; with an empty salt at the prompt it exits naming the salt.
5. `integrity-reveal` on one staging pseudonym with the same salt prints exactly one line.

**Staging may hold prod data.** A restore drill ([runbook 4.1](../runbook.md#41-restore-drill)) leaves real member data there. Before the per-person checks on staging, read the schema comment: `docker exec quiz-staging-db-1 psql -U postgres -d quiz -tAc "SELECT obj_description('public'::regnamespace, 'pg_namespace')"`. If it names a prod dump, run the cohort checks only, or put staging back first. The one exception is `--checks canary` over test accounts, below.

**Staging the canary (before v2.0.0 reaches prod).** The release ships dark, so this is how the mechanics are shown to work. Staging runs on a prod dump, so no per-person check runs over real people there; the dump holds no signals or decoys, which is why it is safe to read `canary` alone.

1. Read the schema comment as above. If staging holds a prod dump, run only `--checks canary`, over test accounts.
2. Set a past `IFS_RULES_1B_FROM` and `IFS_CANARY=true` in staging's `.env` and redeploy. Confirm `decoy_option_id` is set only on daily and counted-mock attempts.
3. On one day, play with about five test accounts only: one that follows the note as an AI would; an honest copier through a translator; one that does not copy; one that reloads; and one client with its signals stripped.
4. Check the rows: `long_copies`, `resumes` and `away_*` are stored and clamped, `pasted` is set on text answers only, and the stripped client has `long_copies` null.
5. The next day run `deploy/integrity-report.sh staging report --since <test day> --until <today> --checks canary`, then, if staging holds no prod data, `--checks people`. Expect pseudonyms only; the void table; `ignored (controls too small)` at this size; no threshold value anywhere in the file; and, with a variable missing from `integrity.env`, the message naming it. A flag, if any, must be a test account.
6. `grep -E '@|[0-9]{6,}' <file>` finds nothing, nor does a grep for each value in `integrity.env`.
7. Repeat with `IFS_RULES_1B_FROM` unset: the command refuses the per-person checks. Put staging's `.env` back.

Whether the checks flag the right people is not something staging shows. That is proven by the planted-profile test (`tests/integration/test_integrity_report.py`: a ring of copiers, a simultaneous copier, key relayers ten minutes and two hours after the leader, an upstream-key follower, a player right on every hard question only, an alumna who copied and must never be printed, honest, practised and strong players whose wrong answers can match the leader's and who must not be flagged, and for the canary a note follower, a heavy tab-switcher, an honest translator user, a player who picks the decoy without copying, and one who forges or strips the totals), and by the unit tests' seeded cohorts of honest players of unequal ability, which must flag no more than chance.

## Rollout and rollback

The wave ships in v2.0.0 with no migration, deployed dark before the rule date.

- Before Madrid midnight of `IFS_RULES_1B_FROM` nothing is collected and the report runs the cohort checks only.
- On prod, `integrity.env` holds all 21 values before the first per-person run; the seven canary and context ones are needed by `canary`, `people` and `split` even while `IFS_CANARY` is off. `g0` and `f` come from the recall pilot. `IFS_CANARY` stays false until the pilot passes and the About line that names the note is live; switching it on later needs no date.
- Rollback is a redeploy of the previous tag; the report is read-only and changes nothing. Unsetting `IFS_RULES_1B_FROM` turns every mechanism off together, and the report then refuses the per-person checks.

## How to run

From the repository on the server (`/srv/quiz/repo`), with the reviewer who holds the salt at the terminal:

```bash
deploy/integrity-report.sh staging report --until 2026-10-12                       # cohort checks (the default)
deploy/integrity-report.sh prod report --until 2026-11-23 --checks all --split 2026-11-09  # per-person: the window starts at the rule date
deploy/integrity-report.sh prod reveal <pseudonym> [<pseudonym>…]                   # the second person's step
```

`report` writes the report to `/dev/shm/quiz-<env>-integrity-<timestamp>.md` (memory, `umask 077`) and prints the path and the `shred -u` reminder; the terminal gets nothing of the report. `reveal` prints to the terminal. Both append `date user mode arguments` to `/srv/quiz/<env>/integrity-history`: never the salt and never a pseudonym.

| Option | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `--until DATE` | **Required.** The last day of the window, excluded; can't be in the future |
| `--since DATE` | First Madrid day included. Cohort-only: default and earliest, the season's 1 September. With a per-person check: default the later of that and the rule date; earlier than the rule date is refused |
| `--checks` | `cohort` (the default: `elo,labels,permutation,keys,fidelity`), `all`, or a comma list of `elo`, `labels`, `permutation`, `keys`, `fidelity`, `follow`, `upstream-key`, `profile`, `home-away`, `canary`, `people`, `split`. `people` needs `follow`, `upstream-key`, `profile`, `home-away` and `canary` and computes them silently if you ask for it alone; `split` computes the canary on the plays before the split as well. Per-person sections run only when named. `--checks canary` alone prints the header and the `canary` section only |
| `--fixed-rank` | The rank the LP replay runs at; default `IFS_INTEGRITY_FIXED_RANK`. Changes only the `replayed@fixed` columns |
| `--split DATE` | With `split`: the families read on the plays before this day alone, and each flagged person's metrics before and after it. Must fall inside the window |

If the report fails with a statement timeout on prod, don't raise the timeout: report it. The fetch reads every attempt of members who played ranked this season (about 100,000 a season), and a missing index is a migration, not a flag.

## Reading each check

Everywhere: `n` is printed next to every rate, share and z, and accuracies carry a Wilson interval `[low, high]` at the configured `z`. A rate on a handful of plays is a rumour. Every per-person z is the one-sided z of an exact (Poisson-binomial) or predictive p-value, never a normal approximation on a handful of rare events, and it never goes below 0: a one-sided test has no evidence to give the other way.

**Small groups in the cohort tables.** Groups are by vertical **or** by position, never crossed. A group with fewer people than `IFS_INTEGRITY_MIN_GROUP` is never printed on its own: small groups are pooled as `other`, and a pool still too small joins the largest group (`Electronics + other`). So every row has at least that many people and the rows of each table cover everyone, and nothing is left out for a subtraction to recover. Two tables over the same people can still single someone out between them (a vertical of four that holds three technical directors, minus the directors' row, is the fourth person), so the report checks every sum and difference of the printed rows, and when one would be fewer than `MIN_GROUP` people the position table is withheld with a line that says so. There is no total row: each table's rows already add up to it. The top 10 composition uses the same vertical groups as its columns.

**`fidelity`** (a cohort line). The report replays every player's ranked plays through the real LP rules, in the order they were graded (including timeouts, hints, repeats, passes, the cushion and comeback, plays that moved nothing because the mock run was uncounted; a daily and a mock question closed by the nightly job at the same instant in the job's order, dailies first; and the bad run each player carried over from the previous season, which the September reset doesn't clear), and says what share of the plays reproduces the stored LP exactly, how many players have a replay mismatch, and how many of those had their position changed by an admin this season (from the audit log). It is the report's own self-test: the `elo` columns below are only as good as this. **Below 100 % is expected on prod**, and the main cause is the nightly difficulty recalibration: the report prices every play at the question's *current* difficulty, options and answer kind, and recalibration rewrites the difficulty of most questions after their day. A position change or a question changed since adds to it. Mismatching players stay in the sample, are counted and never named. Read the number as a trend: if it is far lower than the last time, an approximation has stopped holding and the `elo` and `people` sections should be read with that in mind.

**`elo`: is it the rank, not the people?** (check 0a). Elec plays only. For each vertical group and, separately, each position group it prints people, plays, LP per play as stored, LP per play replayed at the fixed rank, the mean of each person's accuracy with a bootstrap interval, the pooled accuracy with a Wilson interval, the pre-seen plays, and how many people's replay doesn't reproduce their stored LP (`replay mismatch`). LP is Elo-style with a placement head start, so at equal accuracy a Mingo gains and a Technical Director loses. Read:
- *Stored against replayed*: a gap that closes at the fixed rank is the placement artefact.
- *Mean per person* for comparing groups of people (one vote each); *pooled* shows how the group does on the questions but is dominated by the heaviest players.
- *Top 10 composition* under three orderings (stored elec LP, LP at the fixed rank, accuracy lower bound). If mechanics leave the top when the rank is fixed **and** their raw accuracy is not above the electronics members', the symptom is the metric, not cheating. Gate any conclusion on accuracy, not LP order: the artefact explains the ranking, not the accuracy.
- The pre-seen share is printed in its own column: LP earned on questions the player had already seen elsewhere is not dropped silently.
- It never uses `rank.expected` as a baseline, because that is conditioned on rank and would rebuild the artefact it is trying to remove.

**`labels`** (0b). Questions by area, whether a reviewer confirmed the labels, and topic, with the number of elec plays in the window. If most "elec" plays are unreviewed rules-like topics (high-voltage rules, say), the symptom is partly a labelling artefact: have the reviewers work the *Unclassified* queue before suspecting anyone.

**`permutation`** (0c). The mechanics-against-electronics difference in elec accuracy on first-seen plays, with the vertical labels shuffled (seeded, so the same command gives the same `p`). Prints `p`, `n` per side, the rounds and the seed; `p` is `n/a` when a side has fewer than `MIN_GROUP` people (a side of one would be a statement about that person). A `p` above your significance level means the difference is what noise at this `n` produces: wait for more data, fix the metric.

**`keys`** (2b, suspect keys). Single-choice questions whose first answers, from people not passing, late or timed out, come out right *below chance* (the upper Wilson bound under `1/k`). Prints question id, option count, right/n and the interval; never a key or an answer. This one is for the reviewers: open each listed question in Review and correct it. A wrong key also looks like "everyone who knows the topic is wrong", so these questions bias every other check.

**`follow`** (1, follow-the-leader on the daily). Everyone gets the same daily question, which makes two kinds of passing answers measurable. The header says how many pairs each test examined; each test is corrected for that number with Holm at its configured level, so with hundreds of pairs one of them crossing a bar by chance is not a flag.
- **Copy pairs.** Two players who submitted within the configured window of each other, on the questions *both* got wrong: how often they gave the *same* wrong answer, against how the wrong answers of the players outside that window spread (so a ring of copiers doesn't make its own answer look popular). Conditioning on both being wrong takes each player's ability out of it. A copy pair names **both** players: the data says the two are linked, not who copied. Someone reading a teammate's screen can press submit first, so submit order proves nothing; the table prints how often each submitted first and opened the question first as context. The copied person is flagged for provenance too, and that is a reason to talk to both, not an accusation of either.
- **Relays.** An earlier player was wrong, saw the right answer on submitting, and the follower, answering later the same day at *any* delay, was right. The test compares the follower's right answers on those questions with what the follower's own other answers predict (an ability fitted on the rest of their daily answers, each against the question's peer rate, with its uncertainty carried into the p-value), so a follower who always plays after the same friend, or waits two hours, is still tested, and a strong player is right both ways and is not flagged. The leader is printed as `not flagged` unless they are flagged themselves. Questions the follower had seen before (practice, another mode) are left out.
- **Limits.** A relayer who is right on *everything* looks exactly like a strong player: with nothing of their own to compare with, the data can't tell them apart. A player who looks up only the hard questions also shows as following any player who misses those: read a relay flag with the `profile` hard z beside it. Relay inside concurrent mock runs is not tested here.
- **It stops working when the daily becomes per-player.** The shared daily question is what makes it measurable; run this check before that ships.
- Only active members are printed. Alumni and disabled accounts take part (they are part of who answered when) and show as `not active` in a copy pair.

**`upstream-key`** (2, the corrected-key canary). On questions a reviewer corrected (`answer_keys.override` differs from the upstream key), the report regrades each stored answer against the old upstream key and the corrected one with the real grader. A player who matches the *old* key on first exposures after the correction more than the cohort does is reading the bank, not the question. It prints, per pseudonym, exposures, matches, the number expected from the cohort, z (from the exact tail of the matches), and typed-answer matches in their own column (a typed answer matching the old key is stronger evidence than a one-of-four guess; the report doesn't weight them, you do). Limits:
- The set shrinks: `push` drops a correction when the question changes upstream, and the section says how many questions were skipped because no audit time was found.
- **Reviewers are blind to it:** the review screen shows the corrected answer, so a reviewer will not match the old key. Don't read a reviewer's absence from the table as clean.

**`profile`** (3). Printed in this order: the cohort quantiles (p10, p50, p90) of median answer time, below-floor share and the spread of log time, then the person table. Answer times are on first-seen, answered, on-time plays. The floor is the larger of `READING_FLOOR_S` and the question's text length over `READING_CPS`; questions with images are left out of the floor count (the report can't tell how long a figure takes) but stay in the spread. Timing is positive when the below-floor share is high, or when the profile is both narrow (small spread of log time) *and* length-insensitive (answer time not following question length): a person who takes about the same time on a one-line and a three-paragraph question. The **hard z** is a within-person test: the person's ability is fitted on their own first-seen answers to easier questions (difficulty 1 to 3, each against its peer rate), and their right answers on difficulty 4 and 5 are tested against what that ability predicts. A player who is simply stronger than their peers is stronger on both and is not flagged; one who is weak on the easy questions and right on the hard ones (the look-it-up signature) is. It needs `MIN_N` plays on each side. The easy/hard ratio is printed as context with the `n` on each side. If the cohort is itself inflated by copying, the peer rates are too.

**`home-away`** (4). Home area per vertical: Mechanical is `mech`; Electronics, Tractive System and Driverless are `elec`; `rules` is neither home nor away for anyone; verticals without a home are `n/a`. The z is the one-sided difference between a person's residual on away questions and on home ones. A cheater with no domain knowledge is equally good everywhere. It is weak alone (an assistant-using simulated player scored z 1.8) and corroboration only. The vertical is self-editable and the report uses today's; a line says how many people changed vertical since sign-up (a count, not names).

**`canary`** (5, the AI canary). Whether a player's answers land on the decoy after copying a long piece of the question, more than chance. Its own section: [Reading `canary`](#reading-canary). The flag is `reject` in the canary table or the people table; the signals that sit beside it are in [Context columns](#context-columns).

**`people`.** The people worth looking at: active members with at least one positive family. Columns: the pseudonym; one column per family (positive, negative, `insufficient` or `pending`); how many agree; `independent`; `conversation`; then the evidence: plays, first-seen plays, pre-seen plays and the share of positive LP earned on them, timeouts and late as `x/n`, hard z (n hard, n base), away z (n away, n home), below-floor `x/n`, spread, length correlation, follow, upstream z (n), then the canary (`canary hits/trials`: `not collected`, `no trials`, or `hits/trials, expected e, decision`, where the decision is `reject`, `no sign`, `continue`, `ignored (reason)` or `exploratory`) and the context cells ([below](#context-columns)). The footer prints, per family, how many people were eligible, how many were flagged and how many flags chance alone would give: for performance, eligible people times the one-sided tail at each threshold (approximate: both tests are within-person, so they hold for players of any ability, under a one-ability-per-person model); for provenance, an upper bound, the upstream-key chance plus the level of each follow test (Holm keeps the chance of *any* false pair at it, and a copy pair names two people), with the number of copy and relay pairs tested, and the canary's own bound, at most the canary level `α` for up to `M` people over the season (Bonferroni over the fixed `M`), the walk running from the walk start; `n/a` for the share thresholds of timing. If flagged is not clearly above that, the family is noise. The figures are rounded, but they are still functions of the thresholds: treat the footer as private as the rest. Timeouts and late plays are reported but are not an evidence family: a missed daily is a loss, not a sign.

**`split`.** Only with `--split DATE` (use the day the notice went live, or the day the code went public), inside the window. It reads the families again on the plays **before** the split alone, for anyone positive there or over the whole window, so a behaviour that stopped when people were told (or when someone read the code) is not averaged away by the honest-looking rest of the window: `families before` against `families over the window`, with the plays before. Then, for those people, median answer time, below-floor share, accuracy and timeout share before and after, each with `n` and, where it applies, a two-proportion z. Last, a cohort table of how much everyone's median log answer time and below-floor share moved across the split (p10, p50, p90, people), naming no one. It is context for a conversation, never a family of its own. The canary is read on the plays before the split too: those trials are a prefix of the same walk from the walk start, so the flag keeps its guarantee.

## Reading `canary`

**What it measures.** While the decoy is on (`IFS_CANARY`), the page appends a note to a long copy (at least 80 characters) of a ranked single-choice question; the note names the letter of a decoy option ([ADR 0010](../adr/0010-fair-play-and-integrity.md)). The decoy is drawn uniformly over the options shown, from the options table alone, never reading the key. A person who reads the note learns the rule; an AI assistant that follows it picks the decoy. The check asks whether a player picks the decoy after copying more often than an honest reader would. Read whether the canary is usable at all before reading who it flags.

**What the page must do.** The copy arm means something only if the web client:
- appends the note, with this attempt's decoy letter, only when the question payload carries a decoy;
- counts in `long_copies` exactly the copies of at least 80 characters of question text that carried the note;
- resets its counters on a reload, which the server sees as a reopening (`resumes`);
- always sends its totals with the answer.

A copy without the note only dilutes the evidence; a missing total moves the attempt to the unreported arm below. If `IFS_CANARY` is switched off later, new attempts carry no decoy and are no trials. Nudging found after the pilot is handled by raising `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_NUDGE`, not by changing the page.

**A trial.** A ranked play is a trial only if it passes these in order; the first that fails is its void reason, and the section counts the voids:

| Void reason | The play was left out because |
|---|---|
| `not single choice` | the question is not a single-answer choice question (today's answer kind) |
| `options changed` | the decoy or the chosen option is no longer live, the key's options are not all live, or the question changed upstream after the attempt was created, even if a reviewer has since acknowledged the change |
| `too few options` | fewer than three live options |
| `too few wrong options` | fewer than two wrong live options |
| `decoy right` | the decoy is a correct option, so choosing it proves nothing |
| `hint` | a hint was used |
| `not one wrong option` | passed, nothing chosen, several chosen, or the chosen option is correct |

A hit is a trial whose chosen wrong option is the decoy. A key may have several correct options (the set `C`). Wording-only changes void too, which costs power but never biases `p0`; a change to the key alone would leave `p0` exact and voids as well, which is conservative. An acknowledged change still voids: the report reads the question's change mark and the audit log, so clearing the review queue does not bring the trial back.

**The chance rate is exact.** With `n` live options and `C` the correct ones, an honest wrong answer lands on the decoy with chance `p0 = 1/(n − |C|)`. The decoy is drawn uniformly over the options shown, independently of the key and of the player; given that it is outside `C` and the answer is a wrong option, it is uniform over the `n − |C|` wrong options, so a hit has that chance whatever the player's own spread of wrong answers. The report therefore does not model how people go wrong: a per-question wrong-answer distribution would model the player, not the decoy, would be noisier and would be contaminated by the people under test. The same holds for a key corrected later, since `C` is independent of the decoy; only the offered set of options matters. With one correct option the value is `1/(n − 1)`. Questions with 3 options carry about half the evidence per trial of questions with 4, so they move the walk slowly.

**Four arms.** By the attempt's stored totals:
- *Copy*: at least one long copy. The totals are sent with the answer, so the copy came before it, and a reload can only lose copies, never invent one.
- *Placebo*: no long copy **and** no reopening.
- *Unreported*: no long-copy total stored at all (no signals sent: an older page or a tampered client).
- *Neither*: no long copy but a reopening. A reload resets the page's counters, so such an attempt might have copied before the reload. It is in no control and in no decision, but it is walked in the listing below, because a reload costs nothing and loses the copy.

The placebo asks whether people pick the decoy when no note could have told them to. The unreported and neither arms are used only by the listing below; they are kept out of the placebo because the null may not hold for them (a copy may sit behind a reload or a missing total).

**The decision, per person.** The copy-arm trials are walked in the order graded. The null is composite: a person who pastes the question into a translator reads the note too and may be nudged by it, so a hit has chance `p0′ = p0 + g0(1 − p0)` with `g0` = `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_NUDGE`. The alternative is a follower with `p1 = p0 + f(1 − p0)`, `f` = `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_FOLLOW` and `g0 < f`. A hit adds `ln(p1/p0′)` to the walk and a miss adds `ln((1 − p1)/(1 − p0′))`. The likelihood ratio is a supermartingale for every true rate up to `p0′`, so the guarantee holds for any nudge up to `g0`. There is no lower stop: the walk covers every trial, so a person with a bad start is not let off. With `M` = `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_M`, `α` = `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_ALPHA` and `β` = `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_BETA`, the decisions are:
- `reject`: the highest value the walk reached is at least `ln(M/α)`. The boundary is Bonferroni over a fixed per-season `M`, not Holm over whoever happened to have trials in this run, so it does not move between runs: a reject stays reached in a later run of a growing window with the same walk start and the same trials. The trial set is rebuilt from the questions as they are now: a later upstream change to a question, its retirement or a reviewer's key correction removes or reprices the earlier trials on it (the void table shows `options changed`), so a reject reached in one run can vanish in a later one, and a new one can appear. Each run is valid on its own. It is valid at any stopping time, so rereading the report every week costs nothing. `decided at` is the trial count at which the walk first crossed.
- `no sign at f`: not rejected, and the final walk is at or below `ln(β/(1 − α))`. It never clears anyone: it argues only against followers of strength `f` or more, and the canary catches only naive copy-and-paste followers.
- `continue`: everything else.

A `reject` whose final walk has fallen back below the boundary (a run of misses after it crossed) stays a reject and is marked `earlier stretch`. Read both p values then: the anytime `p` says whether the walk ever crossed, the fixed-window `p` says how the whole window looks.

**What is printed per person** (in any canary table): trials; hits; expected (the sum of `p0′`); the fixed-window `p` (the exact Poisson-binomial upper tail at `p0′`, uncorrected, with its z in brackets: context for the size of the pattern, not the basis of the flag); the anytime `p` (the reciprocal of the highest likelihood ratio the walk reached, capped at 1); `decided at`; and the `earlier stretch` note. `decided at` together with the hits lets a reader infer the boundary. That is acceptable only because the output goes to the Technical Directors alone.

**When the canary is not usable.** Gates run in this order, and any that fails turns every decision into `ignored (reason)`:
1. *Exploratory* (below).
2. *Draw check.* Over every decoy play on a single-choice question, whether the decoy fell on a correct option as often as chance gives (`|C|/n` per play), two-sided at `cα/2` per tail (`cα` = `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_CONTROL_ALPHA`). Nobody can influence which letter is the decoy, so `off` points at the implementation or the data, not at a player: `ignored (draw check off)`. It cannot see an option added after the answer; the `options changed` void does that.
3. *Pooled placebo.* The decoy hits of the whole placebo pool against `p0`, with no trimming, two-sided at `cα/2` per tail. A tail counts as `off` only if it stays off after leave-one-out: the single person with the largest excess is removed for the upper tail, the largest deficit for the lower. An excess means the decoy is picked without a note, so after-copy hits can't be read; a deficit means the decoy is observable without the note (the question payload carries it, so a reader can avoid it) and the null is wrong the other way. Either gives `ignored (placebo off)`. The section then says the canary flags no one in this run and is unreliable, and lists up to five pseudonyms by excess hits (hits minus expected) as the top contributors.
4. *Power.* A control that cannot see a leak is not a control. The draw check and the pooled placebo each need the exact power to detect a leak of strength `f` at `cα/2` to be at least `1 − β`, and the placebo needs at least `IFS_INTEGRITY_MIN_GROUP` people. Otherwise every decision is `ignored (controls too small)`, which is what a short or small run prints. A blind control never passes.
5. *Over `M`.* If more people have copy-arm trials than `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_M`, the fixed boundary no longer covers them: `ignored (more players than IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_M)`. The value is not printed.
6. *Own placebo.* A person whose own placebo arm sits above chance at `cα` (one-sided, uncorrected: it only protects the person) is `ignored (own placebo off)`. They stay in the pooled placebo, and their decision is for that person only. A person whose copy walk crosses the boundary while their own placebo is off is not dropped: they are printed in the table "copy walk crossed, own placebo off", with the copy walk and the own placebo tail side by side. It sets no family, so the exclusion removes the flag but not the sight of the person; it is a pointer for a reviewer. Picking the decoy on purpose without copying therefore voids a flag but puts the person in front of one.

On honest data a run comes out blank (a control off by chance) about `2·cα` of the time. A run is judged once: re-running the same window until it is green is not valid. The next scheduled, longer window is.

`ignored` is not a verdict on anyone. With fewer than `IFS_INTEGRITY_MIN_GROUP` placebo contributors the counts print as `fewer than min_group people`, and the verdict still applies.

**Decoy picked without a reported copy.** For each active person the placebo, the unreported and the neither (reloaded) arms are walked together with the same `p0′`, `f` and `ln(M/α)` boundary. A person who crosses is listed with trials, hits, the fixed-window and anytime `p` and the unreported and reloaded shares. The list appears whenever the draw check is ok and the run is not exploratory, even when the pooled or the person's own placebo is off. It sets **no family**: it is a pointer for a reviewer. Either the person read the decoy out of the question payload and picked it, or the client lied (a forged `long_copies = 0`, no signals sent), or the person copied, reloaded the page and answered, which loses the page's totals. Reading the payload takes no AI, so this is a reason to look at the person's context columns and the other families, nothing more.

**Nudge warning.** The copy-arm trials of everyone who is not a `reject` are pooled and tested against `p0′`, upper tail at `cα`. If they exceed it, the section prints that honest readers may be nudged beyond `IFS_INTEGRITY_CANARY_NUDGE`, or that followers are undetected, and to read the canary with extra caution. It is a warning only: no decision changes. The case it guards is the translator and the second-language reader, who read the note as part of the text they were given.

**The walk start.** The guarantee holds for a window that starts at the walk start, `max(rule date, the season's 1 September)`. A later `--since` is a new look at a subset of the same plays: it is `exploratory`, flags no one, lists no one and sets no family, and the section says the window starts after the walk start. A season-long window in 2027-28 starts at 1 September 2027 and is not exploratory. `--split DATE` is different: the part before the split is a prefix of the same walk, so it keeps the guarantee.

**What a flag is and isn't.** A hit is "followed the note", by an AI assistant that obeyed it, or by a person nudged by it beyond what `g0` allows. A `reject` is a reason to look at the context columns and the other families, not a finding. The canary is **provenance**, one family, and only an active person's `reject` feeds it; alone it never starts a conversation (see [the two-family rule](#the-two-family-rule)). It also finds nothing in the common cases: an assistant that ignores the note, a question retyped instead of copied, a copy of fewer than 80 characters, and a copy of a question whose decoy was voided.

**The section, in print order.** Title and explanation; `none in the window` if there are no decoys (for instance with `IFS_CANARY` off); the `exploratory` line; the void table; the draw-check and placebo rows with the number of own-placebo exclusions; the line saying the canary flags no one when a gate is off, with the top placebo contributors; the person table, for active members with `reject` only (pseudonym, then the numbers above and the context cells); the table "decoy picked without a reported copy"; the table "copy walk crossed, own placebo off"; the nudge warning if it fired; the caution line; the footer with `m` (people with copy-arm trials) and the counts of each decision.

## Context columns

The per-attempt totals the page sends with a ranked answer, summed per person over their counted ranked plays in the window that carry them. They are **context**: they print for people already in the `people` table or a canary table, and never put anyone there, never set a family and never count towards the two-family rule. Each cell carries its `n`.

| Column | What it counts |
|---|---|
| `undeclared away any/over/n, median` | Of the `n` answers that reported it: how many had any undeclared time away from the question, how many had more than `IFS_INTEGRITY_AWAY_UNDECLARED_S`, and the median (seconds) among those with some |
| `declared away any/n, median` | The same for time away that the page counted as a look-up opened from the question itself (the rulebook panel or a translation link) |
| `pasted/text n` | Free-text answers into which something was pasted, out of the text answers that reported it |
| `long copies attempts/n` | Attempts with at least one long copy of the question, out of those that reported it, with the total |
| `resumes attempts/n` | Attempts the server saw reopened while running (a reload or a second tab), out of all with signals, with the total |
| `decoy attempts without totals/n` | Decoy attempts with no stored long-copy total, out of the person's decoy attempts: no signals sent. The server still counts the reopenings of such an attempt |

- **`not collected`.** Signals exist only for answers created on or after Madrid midnight of `IFS_RULES_1B_FROM` on a daily or a counted mock attempt; before it, from a client that doesn't send them, and after they are nulled 90 days after the season ends, the cell says so. An attempt started before midnight of the rule date is dropped even if it was submitted after it: the approximation errs towards not using it. Uncounted mock attempts have no signals at all.
- **Forgeable.** The page reports them; a modified client can send zeros or nothing. A low value proves nothing. The reopenings are counted by the server, but only on the paths the web app uses ([security.md](../security.md#threats-and-controls)).
- **Allowed behaviour produces them.** Every table that prints context is followed by a line saying so: the rulebook in another tab, a translator, notifications and reading in a second language all show as time away, pastes or long copies. A heavy tab-switcher, or a player who pastes the question into a translator, is the typical false reading.
- **They set no family.** The plan filed them under timing and behaviour; here they are not filed under any family: `families()` never reads them, so they can't be one half of the two-family rule, alone or beside anything. That is deliberately stricter, and it can be relaxed only by a decision recorded in the ADR.
- **The cohort table.** `signals across the cohort` prints, for each share above, the p50 over people, rounded to 5 points, and `n/a` below `IFS_INTEGRITY_MIN_GROUP` people. The p90 is printed only with at least 10 contributors (`P90_MIN`, a display privacy constant, not a decision threshold: with fewer, the p90 is nearly one person). It names no one, prints no threshold value, and is where to read what ordinary play looks like here before setting the away threshold.

## The two-family rule

The families are `performance` (hard-item residual, away residual), `timing` (below-floor share, narrow and length-insensitive profile), `provenance` (follow-the-leader, upstream-key following, the canary) and `supervised` (the in-room benchmark, always `pending` here). A person is "worth a conversation" only when **two or more families agree**, and a family with too few plays is `insufficient`, which is not a "no". The `independent` column says whether provenance or supervised is among them. Only an active person's canary `reject` is positive evidence, and it is the only canary outcome that sets a family: `no sign at f`, `continue`, `ignored`, `exploratory` and the "decoy picked without a reported copy" list all leave provenance unset (it is never negative). A person with the canary alone has one family and is not a conversation; the canary with timing is. The context columns ([above](#context-columns)) are filed under no family and **never** count, alone or with anything else.

The known weakness: performance and timing come from the same plays and share causes (a player who knows the answers in advance looks fast *and* good). Two families that are both performance and timing are weaker evidence than one of each side with provenance. Provenance is not free of it either: a player who looks up the hard questions can also show as a relay after players who miss them, and a copy pair puts the copied person in provenance too. The chance counts in the footer make that visible; the Technical Directors should decide whether ADR 0010 should require provenance or supervised among the two before a conversation. What to raise with them is the person's pseudonym, which families fired and the `n` behind each, never a name.

## The two-person reveal

The operator (the maintainer running the script) reads the pseudonyms in the report. The reviewer who holds the salt reveals them, together with the operator or another Technical Director, with `deploy/integrity-report.sh <env> reveal <pseudonym>…`, which prints `pseudonym | user id | display name` for the pseudonyms named and nothing else (`--include-alumni` also looks among alumni; an unknown pseudonym exits 1). Agree on who is revealed *before* anyone says a name. The separation is **procedural**: a maintainer with server access and the salt could map everyone, and the operator has read access to every table. It holds because the salt is typed by someone else, per review, and not stored.

## Afterwards

1. `shred -u /dev/shm/quiz-<env>-integrity-<timestamp>.md` (the script printed it). Keep any notes pseudonymous, or none.
2. Destroy the salt. The next review gets a new one.
3. The `integrity-history` line is all that remains on the server: who ran what and when.
4. Close the review in the Technical Directors' notes (outcome, thresholds used, date). Anything that follows is a conversation between people, outside this tool.

## Known approximations

- **Current question data.** Difficulty, options, answer kind and texts are the current ones, not the values at the time of the answer (difficulty is recalibrated nightly; options can retire; a correction can change the answer kind). The `fidelity` line shows how much of the replay still holds.
- **Reviewer blind spot** in the upstream-key check, and a corrected-key set that shrinks.
- **Canary: current options, and what changed since.** A trial reads the question's present key and live options. An option added upstream after the attempt would make `p0` too small (anti-conservative); the `options changed` void removes those plays from the question's upstream change mark and from the audit log, so it survives a reviewer's acknowledgement. It also voids wording-only changes (a cost in power) and key-only changes (conservative). The exact alternative, the option's own creation time, needs a migration and is not built; the draw check can't see an added option.
- **Canary: totals are forgeable and counted per page.** `long_copies` is a client total, a reload resets it (the placebo arm excludes plays with a reopening for that reason, and the listing walks them) and a modified client sends what it likes, including zero or nothing. Forged or stripped totals end in the unreported or the placebo arm, and a reload (no modified client needed) in the neither arm; in all three a follower who picks the decoy shows in the "decoy picked without a reported copy" list; a follower who sends honest totals and avoids the decoy is not caught, which is the weakness of any canary. An attempt started before Madrid midnight of the rule date is dropped even if submitted after it.
- **Canary: the decoy is in the question payload.** The timed question carries the decoy option id (the page needs it for the note), so a reader can pick the decoy on purpose, which the list makes visible, or avoid it, which only weakens the canary for that person (a placebo deficit shows it in aggregate).
- **Canary: a coalition.** Several people each below their own placebo threshold can together push the pooled placebo off. Leave-one-out stops one heavy contributor from doing it alone; a coalition fails closed (no one is flagged, the section lists the top contributors) and is a signal to look at them, not a clean bill.
- **Canary: a late start is paid for.** The walk is cumulative from the walk start, so a player who starts following after a stretch of honest copy trials needs net hits that make up for the misses. Restart points with a Bonferroni over their number would recover the power and are not built.
- **Canary: nudged readers above `g0`.** An honest reader nudged by the note more than the configured nudge looks like a follower. Only the warn-only copy-arm check and the pilot guard it.
- **Canary: blank runs.** On honest data about `2·cα` of the runs come out blank; a blank run is not repeated until it is green.
- **Relay inside mock runs** and **practice-then-play** are not follow evidence; a question seen in practice or another mode first is `pre-seen` and kept out of the performance, timing, relay and upstream-key tests (it is reported in its own column).
- **A relayer right on everything** is indistinguishable from a strong player, and **who copied whom** is not in the data: a copy pair names both.
- **Everyone with ranked play this season is analysed**, including people who hid themselves from the boards and alumni; the privacy notice written with ADR 0010 says so. Only active members appear in the people table.
- **Statistical power.** At this sample the report finds heavy, consistent patterns (a copier, a bank follower) and misses light use. It also needs the thresholds to be right for this team, which is why they are calibrated on the team's own distribution and not on a simulation.
- **A white-box player** who reads this page and the public code can shape their behaviour; the thresholds are private for that reason. The relay test has no time window to wait out and copy pairs don't care who pressed submit first, and `split` reads the families on the plays before a date of your choice, so what someone did before they adapted still shows.
- **The output is pseudonymous data, which is personal data.** Treat it as such, and delete it.
